"ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — which one is actually best?"

It's the question I get asked most, and also the hardest to answer directly, because the answer depends on **what you do day to day**.
So instead of comparing spec sheets, this post sorts them by the kind of work you do.

<!-- truncate -->

## The short answer

| If you… | Main pick |
| --- | --- |
| Aren't sure and want the safest choice | **ChatGPT** |
| Read long documents or write long pieces every day | **Claude** |
| Live in Gmail and Google Docs | **Gemini** |
| Work at a company that uses Microsoft 365 | **Copilot** (plus one of the above) |
| Mostly look things up and need sources | **Perplexity** (alongside any of the above) |

**All three have free versions, and they're plenty for getting started.**

## But before you choose: pick just one

The most common place beginners get stuck is opening five accounts, using each a little, and never getting comfortable with any of them.

**Choose one as your main assistant and use it for two full weeks before deciding anything.**
How skilled you are with AI matters far more than which company you use.

## Their personalities

### ChatGPT: the all-purpose assistant with the most features

It has the most complete ecosystem, with image generation, data analysis and voice conversation all well integrated.
There are also more tutorials and examples online for it than for any other, so when you hit a problem, answers are easy to find.

**Good for:** people who aren't sure what they need and want one tool that can do a bit of everything.

**Watch out for:** more features also means a busier interface, and beginners can easily get lost in the options. Start with just the basic chat box.

### Claude: the senior advisor who writes well and reads fast

It's especially strong with long documents — you can paste in an entire report and ask it to analyze it.
Its writing reads naturally and is clearly structured, its editing and rewriting is consistently good,
and it tends to be honest about when it's unsure.

**Good for:** people who read lots of documents and write lots of text every day.

**Watch out for:** it doesn't have as many add-ons and plugins as ChatGPT.

### Gemini: the assistant that already lives in your Google account

It's deeply integrated with Gmail, Google Docs, Sheets and Drive,
so it can work directly with files in your Drive, and it's handy for looking up the latest information.

**Good for:** people who do almost all their work inside Google's tools.

**Watch out for:** if you're using a work account, check your company's IT policy first.

## Sort it by the work you do

### You write lots of emails and copy every day

→ Claude or ChatGPT are both great. **What matters isn't the tool — it's whether you've built your own prompt templates.**

### You read reports, research papers or contracts every day

→ **Claude**. Understanding and summarizing long documents is its clearest strength.

### You often need the latest information

→ Use **Perplexity** as your starting point, then hand its answer to your main assistant to shape into the format you need.

### You need to organize data or do spreadsheet calculations

→ **ChatGPT's data analysis feature**. It actually runs code to do the math, which is far more accurate than reasoning in plain text.

:::tip
Remember: AI is "predicting text," not "doing math."
Anything that needs precise calculation should go through a feature that actually computes it — or you should double-check it yourself.
:::

### Your company already uses Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace

→ Start with what your company provides, since data compliance has already been taken care of.
Then set up a separate personal assistant for tasks that don't involve company data.

## Is the free version enough?

For most people the answer is: **at first, yes. Once you get comfortable, no.**

Common limits on free plans: caps on how often you can use the stronger models, smaller allowances for advanced features, and slower responses at busy times.

**My advice:** stick with the free version until you're **hitting the usage limit more than twice a week**, then think about paying.
By then you'll also know which features you actually need.

## Before switching, make sure it's not how you're asking

Plenty of people decide "this AI isn't any good," switch to another, and find it's just the same. Before you switch, check:

1. Did you give it enough background?
2. Did you say clearly what format you want?
3. Did you give it an example?
4. Have you tried switching to a reasoning model?

If you've done all four and it still isn't working, then think about switching. The how-to is in
[6 principles for writing good prompts](/blog/how-to-write-good-ai-prompt).

## Data safety: more important than which one you pick

Whichever you choose, this rule applies:

> **If you wouldn't want a coworker, client or reporter to see it, don't paste it into AI.**

Client lists, unpublished quotes, personal information, passwords — never paste any of these.
For the full explanation, see [Safety & Judgment](/resources/safety).
