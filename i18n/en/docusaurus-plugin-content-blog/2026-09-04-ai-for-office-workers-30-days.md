The biggest trap in learning AI is treating it like a course you have to find extra time for.

What actually works is the opposite: **use AI directly on the work you already have to do.**
This 30-day plan is built on that idea. Fifteen minutes a day, all spent on real tasks.

<!-- truncate -->

## How to use this plan

- One task per day. **Use something you genuinely need to get done this week**, not a made-up exercise
- It's fine if you fall behind. The order matters more than the pace
- Every time a prompt works well, **save it to your notes right away**

---

## Week 1: Say what you mean

**Goal:** Learn to give enough background that AI gets it right the first time.

| Day | What to do today |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Pick one main AI assistant — just one. Ask it three questions you're actually thinking about today |
| 2 | Re-ask yesterday's worst question using "role × task × context × format," and compare the results |
| 3 | Find an email you've been struggling to write and ask for three versions in different tones |
| 4 | Find a long document you haven't had time to read and ask it to pull out three key points "for someone in my role" |
| 5 | Ask it to ask you 3–5 questions before answering, and see what it's like when it checks first and then gets to work |
| 6 | Deliberately ask a question in an area you know well, and find where it gets things wrong |
| 7 | Review: save the three prompts that worked best this week as templates |

**This week's mindset:** You're not "giving commands." You're handing off work to a new coworker.

## Week 2: Tackle what you really do every day

**Goal:** Find your most frequent tasks and let AI take the first draft.

| Day | What to do today |
| --- | --- |
| 8 | Turn a meeting recording into a transcript, then ask AI to organize it into decisions plus a to-do list |
| 9 | Take some messy data and ask it to organize it into a Markdown table |
| 10 | Try the "give an example" technique: paste three good pieces you've written and ask it to match them |
| 11 | Ask it to play three different roles and critique a proposal you're working on |
| 12 | Boil a long document down to a version your manager can read in 60 seconds |
| 13 | Pick a chore you don't want to do and try handing the whole thing over |
| 14 | Review: estimate how much time you saved this week, and what you did with it |

**This week's mindset:** Look for tasks that happen every single week. That's where your effort pays off.

## Week 3: Build your judgment

**Goal:** Know when not to trust it, and what information must never be pasted in.

| Day | What to do today |
| --- | --- |
| 15 | Ask it for three sources, then open each one to check |
| 16 | Learn to push back: "Which parts are you confident about, and which are guesses?" |
| 17 | Add "If you're not sure, say you don't know" to all your templates |
| 18 | Check the settings of the AI service you use and confirm its data-use policy |
| 19 | List the information from your job that must **never** be pasted into AI |
| 20 | Practice anonymizing: turn a real document into a version with code names, then use that |
| 21 | Review: write down three things you won't hand to AI, and why |

**This week's mindset:** AI handles drafts and speed; you handle the facts and the responsibility. Your name is the one on it.

→ Related reading: [Hallucinations and fact-checking](/blog/ai-hallucination-and-fact-check) · [Safety & Judgment](/resources/safety)

## Week 4: Turn one-offs into something repeatable

**Goal:** Lock in what worked as templates and processes.

| Day | What to do today |
| --- | --- |
| 22 | Go through the prompts you've saved this month and sort them into 5 folders |
| 23 | Rewrite your three most-used prompts as fill-in-the-blank templates |
| 24 | Try a "custom assistant" or "project" feature and set up your standing rules in it |
| 25 | Upload documents you refer to often and build a small knowledge base |
| 26 | Use the knowledge base to answer a question, and compare it with a regular chat |
| 27 | Pick something you do every week and map out its steps |
| 28 | Evaluate: is it worth automating? (Happens weekly + clear rules + mistakes are easy to fix) |
| 29 | Turn one task into a standard process and write down the step-by-step routine |
| 30 | Final review: how many hours did you save this month? What do you want to tackle next month? |

**This week's mindset:** Templates give you a far better return than automation — and they don't need any technical skills.

## Where you'll be after 30 days

If you really work through it, you should have:

- Your own prompt notes, ready to copy whenever a similar task comes up
- At least two routine tasks that now take noticeably less time
- A reflex to check any numbers or citations AI gives you
- A clear sense of what information you can't paste in, and why
- A knowledge base, or one process that runs the same way every time

That's pretty much everything in stages 1 and 2 of the [Roadmap](/roadmap).

## One last reminder

After a month, look back and ask one thing: **what did you do with the time you saved?**

If the answer is "watched more AI tutorials," you've got it backwards.
The point of learning AI is to give you time back for the things that matter more.
