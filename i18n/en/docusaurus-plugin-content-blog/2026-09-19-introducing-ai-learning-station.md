> "I know AI is important, but where do I actually start?"

For a while now, AI anxiety has been spreading through my circle of friends and family. The people asking come from marketing, HR, sales, education, healthcare, accounting — just about every field.
They don't lack information. If anything, they're drowning in it.

<!-- truncate -->

## The problem isn't a lack of resources — it's a lack of order

Open YouTube or social media and there are more AI tutorials than you could ever watch. But look closely and you'll notice a few common problems:

**First, most tutorials assume you have a technical background.** They jump straight into APIs, model parameters and deployment.
For someone who spends their day making slides and writing emails, every individual word makes sense, but put together, it's impossible to follow.

**Second, there are endless tool recommendations, but nobody tells *you* which one to use.** One article lists 30 AI tools,
and you usually come away more anxious than before, because you can't tell which ones have anything to do with you.

**Third, prompts copied from articles often don't fit.** Those templates were written to show off,
not for the situations you actually face at work.

**Fourth, explanations of how AI works are either too shallow or too deep.** Either they just shout "AI is amazing, learn it now!"
or they throw research papers at you. The middle ground — "explaining why it answers the way it does, in words a regular person can follow" — is something very few people write.

## That gap is what AI Learning Station is here to fill

This site is designed around one goal: **to give someone with no technical background a clear path to follow.**

Specifically:

### A roadmap with a clear order

The [Roadmap](/roadmap) splits learning AI into three stages, and each stage spells out
what to learn, how far you need to get before moving on, and where to go next. You don't have to figure out what to study on your own.

### A starting point that gets you results in 20 minutes

[Get Started](/start) skips the theory and has you try things right away.
After five steps, you'll have a result you can genuinely use, plus a method you can repeat.

### Things you can copy straight away

Every template in the [Prompt Library](/resources/prompts) can be pasted directly into AI —
just replace the parts in brackets with your own details. There are templates for marketing, HR, admin, sales, teachers and students.

### Being honest about the limits

AI makes mistakes, makes things up, and some things shouldn't be handed to it at all.
The [Safety & Judgment](/resources/safety) and [Hallucinations and fact-checking](/resources/ai-basics/hallucination)
sections exist for one reason: I didn't want to build a site that only says nice things about AI.

## Three writing principles

When I built this site, I set myself three rules:

**One: write for non-engineers.** Every technical term gets explained first, and wherever an everyday analogy will do, there's no math.
Coding is always optional, never required.

**Two: every post has to answer "So what should I do now?"**
If you finish reading and there's nothing you can go and try, the post doesn't make the cut.

**Three: no exaggeration.** I won't write anything beyond the facts just to make the content look impressive,
and I'll tell you clearly which things you shouldn't hand to AI.

## Where to start

If you've never used AI at all, start with [Get Started](/start). It takes 20 minutes.

If you've used it a few times but didn't find it very useful, my guess is the problem is how you're asking.
Go straight to [6 principles for writing good prompts](/blog/how-to-write-good-ai-prompt).

If you'd rather understand how it works before diving in, start with [AI Basics](/resources/ai-basics).
